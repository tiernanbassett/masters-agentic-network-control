# Literature Overview

This file provides an overview of the academic papers reviewed throughout the dissertation.

| Paper | Research Area | Relevance to Dissertation |
|---|---|---|
| A Survey on Large Language Model Based Autonomous Agents — Wang et al. | LLM-based autonomous agents | Provides foundational understanding of agent construction, applications, evaluation methods, and challenges. Does not directly establish suitability for wireless network control. |
| 6G Needs Agents: Toward Agentic AI-Native Networks for Autonomous Intelligence — Ferrag et al. (2026) | Agentic AI-native 6G networks | Proposes an agentic AI-native network architecture and evaluates LLM reasoning, latency and computational efficiency. Relevant to agent-controller separation and safety, but does not experimentally demonstrate closed-loop network control or compare against conventional network-control baselines. |
| Understanding O-RAN: Architecture, Interfaces, Algorithms, Security, and Research Challenges — Polese et al. (2023) | O-RAN architecture and intelligent wireless network control | Explains O-RAN architecture, including RICs, xApps, rApps, and open interfaces. Relevant to control timescales, interoperability, and conflict mitigation. Reviews existing research but does not directly demonstrate that AI-based RIC control outperforms conventional optimisation. |
| Scheduling Algorithms for 5G Networks and Beyond: Classification and Survey — Mamane et al. (2022) | Radio resource management and 5G scheduling algorithms | Explains how radio resources are allocated, the different scheduling strategies, and their performance metrics and trade-offs. Provides potential conventional baselines for evaluating agentic network control, depending on the selected control task. |
